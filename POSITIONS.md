# Positions of the W-Structure

The positions of the author used in the works. A position is an axiom of the theory; everything else in the works is derived from positions, computed by programs, or marked as a candidate or an open problem. Where a work refines a position, the latest wording is given.

## Change, space and time

1. One indivisible change is one step and one tick. Length is the number of steps, time is the number of ticks. The speed c is one step per tick.
2. A link is a stable sequence of acts of change (movements of points). The arrangement is dynamic: a state is a place in a sequence, not a snapshot. No point is ever at rest.
3. Local self-closure is an exchange of places of neighbouring points. An exchange is an indivisible act: one step, one tick. Its side is a property of the act. The path of an exchange (a loop) has length one step; there is no metric inside a step.
4. Points have nonzero size, one step. The arrangement of places forms no lattice.
5. A closed system as a whole does not change (conservation laws).
6. Indistinguishable things cannot carry different weight.
7. There are no probabilities: a transition to the next configuration happens with probability 1. Locally the previous state can be computed but not restored.
8. Three levels of motion: the exchange of neighbours; the circulation of a particle; the global figure-eight that every point of the whole performs. Everything is counted from the direction of the global eight: it fixes which sign of charge counts as matter.

## Energy and mass

9. E = ħω and λ = h/p.
10. At every step the budget of change is shared between translation and internal change in quadrature: E² = (mc²)² + (pc)², and E = mc² at rest.
11. Mass is the density of self-closed links, counted in the particle's own ticks. Its value is set by the large scale (levels, global eights), not by the local dynamics of a particle core.

## Particles

12. A point closes on itself by one loop. A neutrino closes on itself by a figure-eight, the smallest possible self-closure.
13. The electron is three points A–O–B with two loops of opposite orientation; its central point O is an eight contracted into a point; the whole electron is an eight through its three points — one eight on two levels. The proton is four points A–P₁–P₂–C with three loops (+, −, +). Both are one-dimensional objects in three-dimensional space. The muon is a cross; the tau is a cross with a perpendicular eight.
14. The closure element is the element common to the eights of a particle: the point O for the electron, the exchange P₁↔P₂ for the proton.
15. Rule 2:1: the orientation of a particle returns after two cycles.
16. The internal exchange of a particle core is itself the step of its circulation.
17. Charge is the direction of a loop relative to the circulation of its particle. The photon is a point without particle self-closure moving one step per tick.
18. The hydrogen atom is seven points and five loops. The electron cannot pass through the proton; it changes sides by exchange of places. The bound atom is a system of its own, not a sum of parts; the element common to the electron and the proton is the link between them.

## Gravity and black holes

19. Gravity is a property of the medium: a gradient of the rate of time.
20. From a form, continuations of its self-closed links go into the medium; they are sequences of exchanges, they move with the form, and they are not mass.
21. The rate of steps is constant at every local point; seen from outside, a step in a densified region is longer and there are more steps per unit path — two manifestations of one densification with the same factor.
22. Distance is counted in layers; densification adds steps inside the cells of a layer, not new cells.
23. Counts and densities of unlinked configurations add linearly.
24. A black hole is the limit 3W: all possible links are realized and nothing distinguishable changes inside; there is no singularity, and the core has no mass. A link that reaches the limit 3W does not disappear; the limit only grows.
